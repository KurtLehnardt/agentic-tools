I want to build a personal finance app. Can you validate whether there's a market for this?

Here's the research I found:

## Broad Query: "personal finance app"
- Volume: 45,000/month (Estimated), declining
- Competitors: YNAB ($15/month, 4.6★), Every Dollar ($12/month, 4.5★), Mint (acquired/shut down), Empower, Personal Capital
- SERP composition: Listicles dominate (8+ comparison articles), only 2 actual tools on page 1
- Subscores (if broad): D=8 (high volume), S=3 (saturated, many strong tools), M=8, B=7 = 1,344 (viable but risky)

## Narrow Slice: "freelancer income tracking app"
- Volume: 8,200/month (Estimated), rising
- Problem: Freelancers need to track income by client (for invoicing, taxes), not general spending like employees
- Competitors: Wave ($0 free, weak, no client-by-client analytics), generic personal finance apps (not designed for 1099 model), spreadsheets
- SERP composition: 2 listicles, mostly people asking "how do I track invoices", no dedicated tool dominates
- Subscores (if narrow): D=7 (high rising), S=8 (open gap), M=9 (clear payer: freelancer), B=8 = 4,032 (strong opportunity)

Incumbent weaknesses in the narrow slice:
- YNAB is employee/household-focused, no client-by-client breakdown
- Wave is free but missing critical features (no retention invoicing, poor reporting, no mobile)
- Spreadsheets are manual, error-prone, tax-audit risky

The broad idea is crowded. Is there a market if I narrow it?
